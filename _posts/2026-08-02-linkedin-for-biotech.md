Biotech companies often treat LinkedIn as an announcement board: a conference, a new hire, a publication, an awareness day, then silence until the next event.

Those posts are useful, but they rarely build a sustained reason to follow the company. Engagement grows when an organisation develops a recognisable point of view and returns to it consistently.

## Start with editorial territory

An editorial territory is the set of subjects your organisation has both the authority and the reason to discuss. It sits at the intersection of your expertise, your audience’s questions, and your strategic goals.

For a life-science centre, that might include translational infrastructure, collaboration across resident companies, and the practical needs of international delegations. For a medtech company, it might be clinical workflow, evidence generation, and the experience of the professionals who use the device.

The territory should be narrow enough to feel distinctive and broad enough to support months of useful content.

## Create repeatable formats

Consistency becomes easier when the team is not inventing a new format every week. A small number of recurring series can do much of the work:

- A scientist or resident spotlight.
- One research idea explained clearly.
- A behind-the-scenes view of a process.
- A short response to an industry development.
- A practical lesson from a project or event.

The repetition is a strength. It helps the audience recognise what they will get and helps the internal team gather material efficiently.

## Make expertise visible, not abstract

“Our people are our greatest asset” tells the reader very little. A specific observation from a scientist, founder, programme lead, or technical specialist shows expertise in action.

Ask one focused question. Edit the response for clarity while preserving the speaker’s voice. Give the reader enough context to understand why the perspective matters.

This approach creates content that is human without drifting away from the organisation’s scientific substance.

## Measure the right signals

Likes are easy to see, but they are not the only useful measure. Depending on the goal, pay attention to:

- Saves and shares, which suggest practical value.
- Profile and website visits, which indicate curiosity.
- Comments from relevant people, which show resonance within the field.
- Direct enquiries and conversations influenced by the content.
- Growth among the audiences the organisation actually wants to reach.

A high-performing post is helpful. A consistent body of credible content is more valuable. It compounds into familiarity, trust, and a clearer picture of what the organisation stands for.
